# Millot HaHigayon — Name Classification Research Record

## Research question

Do the name classifications described by the source form a structure in which three initial classes are genera shared by all languages, followed by six more specific species where language-level differences actually appear?

## Current working model

The researcher states that:
1. the first three name classes are shared across all languages;
2. therefore those first three function as genera, not as language-specific species;
3. the full name system then divides into six species at a more specific level;
4. investigation of language differences belongs primarily at the species level where the variation begins;
5. the common three remain necessary because they establish what kind of name/reference is under discussion.

## Why this matters methodologically

If a class is common to all relevant languages, investigating it as though it were a language-specific species produces artificial distinctions.

Conversely, stopping at the common genus level hides the variation that appears below it.

Therefore:

genus = domain identification

species = localized differentiation

investigation = concentration at the differentiation point

## Relation to the five predicables

This name-classification system must not automatically be identified with the five predicables.

The working hypothesis is that:
- five predicables classify a predicational relationship between a universal/term and a subject;
- name classes classify a mode of signification/reference;
- the six species therefore may operate on a different axis.

This is a hypothesis until the exact source text is extracted.
