# Crypto Economics & Token Design

**Status:** PLANNED — research and specification library; no token launch or economic performance claim is implied.

## Research modules

- **ECON-01 — Purpose and utility:** What the unit represents, what users can do with it, and why it is needed rather than an ordinary account credit or payment method.
- **ECON-02 — Participants and value flows:** Users, contributors, maintainers, service operators, treasury, and other roles; identify who pays and who receives value.
- **ECON-03 — Supply and allocation:** Issuance rules, caps (if any), allocation, vesting, unlocks, burns (if relevant), and governance over parameter changes.
- **ECON-04 — Incentives and contribution rewards:** Eligibility, quality measures, duplicate contributions, maintenance, review, appeals, and abuse resistance.
- **ECON-05 — Treasury and sustainability:** Funding sources, operating costs, reward budget, reserves, reporting, and failure scenarios.
- **ECON-06 — Demand hypotheses:** Testable reasons for usage; distinguish product adoption from speculative demand.
- **ECON-07 — Stress tests:** Low adoption, high contribution volume, low liquidity, concentration, governance capture, and reward-budget exhaustion.
- **ECON-08 — Alternatives analysis:** Compare a transferable token with non-transferable credits, direct fiat payments, subscriptions, and marketplace fees.
- **ECON-09 — Economic invariants:** Constraints that must hold, assumptions that can change, and tests that detect invalid or unstable parameters.
- **ECON-10 — Disclosure and risk:** Plain-language explanation of uncertainty, dependencies, conflicts, and the absence of guaranteed returns.

## Acceptance criteria

A model is not ready for external claims until assumptions, equations, parameters, test cases, failure modes, and source evidence are documented and independently reviewable. A token's existence or market price must never be inferred from a model or proposal.
