# Algorithmic & Neural Governance Office

**Status:** SPECIFIED / PROPOSED INSTITUTE-NATIVE ARCHITECTURE

## Purpose

The Algorithmic & Neural Governance Office (ANGO) is a dedicated functional department of the proposed Institute architecture for governing, evaluating, verifying, and documenting algorithmic and neural computational systems.

It is intentionally modeled as a **distinct Institute capability**, rather than as a conventional ministry, regulator, central bank, or generic AI office.

The architecture treats algorithmic/neural governance as a dedicated control and evidence domain spanning:

- algorithmic systems;
- neural models;
- model behavior and drift;
- reasoning and inference;
- agents and agentic systems;
- evidence and provenance;
- verification and audit;
- authorization boundaries;
- deployment consequences;
- inter-system dependencies.

## Innovation boundary

The Institute may describe this as a **proposed novel architectural category**: a dedicated Algorithmic & Neural Governance Office integrated with Vitruvius, Evidence/Provenance, Verification, Drift Forensics, Rational Logic, NTM, Policy/Authorization, and Integration.

The statement that this exact institutional form exists nowhere in government should **not** be treated as a verified global fact until a systematic comparative institutional survey establishes it. The stronger defensible claim is that this is a new Institute-native architecture proposed by WANGA and not merely a conventional government-office label.

## Relationship to Rational Logic and NTM

Rational Logic remains a protected proprietary reasoning substrate.

The Neural Thinking Machine (NTM) is the inference-time computational/reasoning layer.

The Algorithmic & Neural Governance Office governs the **evidence, verification, authorization, lifecycle, and accountability boundary around those computational capabilities**.

It therefore does not claim ownership of external models or systems.

## Relationship to Vitruvius

Vitruvius provides architecture intelligence:

```
Repository / System
    -> Architecture
    -> Components
    -> Dependencies
    -> Lineage
    -> Operational Context
    -> Evidence Gaps
    -> Vitruvius Graph
```

The Algorithmic & Neural Governance Office consumes these mappings for governance analysis.

Vitruvius maps architecture; it does not independently grant authority.

## External corpus routing

The Office becomes a destination for relevant sources from:

- governments and public institutions;
- international organizations;
- central banks and financial institutions;
- insurers;
- AI companies;
- open-source AI projects;
- universities and research institutes;
- standards organizations;
- industrial and infrastructure systems.

External repositories are evidence sources for architecture research. Their presence in the corpus does not imply affiliation, endorsement, control, or participation in the Institute.

## Proposed responsibilities

1. Maintain the algorithmic/neural governance taxonomy.
2. Map model and agent lifecycle states.
3. Define evidence requirements for consequential AI systems.
4. Coordinate drift-forensics inputs.
5. Route architecture findings through Vitruvius.
6. Maintain verification and authorization interfaces.
7. Track unresolved evidence and provenance gaps.
8. Coordinate cross-office algorithmic dependencies.
9. Produce research metrics and comparative architecture reports.
10. Maintain a re-verification cycle as external systems change.
